**LEUCINE**

Amino acids form chains that become the proteins central to the development of muscle tissue,
skin, and bone. They also play an important part in the bodily functions ancillary to muscle
building: energy production, post-workout recovery, fat loss, healthy digestion, mood, and brain
function. No chemical reaction takes place within the body without the aid of amino acids.

Overall, the body utilizes 22 standard amino acids for protein. The human body can synthesize
13 of these amino acids (non-essential amino acids; NEAAs) on its own and, therefore, doesn’t
require outside sources, such as food or supplements, to obtain them. The remaining 9 amino
acids are classified as “essential” (or “indispensable”) because they can only be obtained by
external sources like food or supplements.

A closer look at the 9 EAAs shows three are classified as branched chain amino acids (BCAAs)
based on their molecular structure: leucine, isoleucine, and valine. BCAAs are the most effective
EAAs at reducing existing muscle breakdown and building new muscle after intense exercise. Of
all the amino acids that help develop muscle tissue, the three BCAAs are responsible for as much
as 35% of this production. Additionally, the presence of high amounts of BCAAs in the
bloodstream during intense exercise slows the breakdown of energy (catabolism) and boosts the
creation of energy (anabolism) during recovery.

During strenuous exercise, leucine is the BCAA that depletes most rapidly, thus making it the
most crucial to replenish. Specifically, blood leucine levels decline by about 30% during both
strength training and cardio workouts. Supplementing with leucine to increase availability has
several benefits related to muscle protein synthesis (MPS). Leucine activates the main muscle
building pathway, mTOR, for up to 24 hours post-workout. Furthermore, as people age, more
leucine is typically needed to keep the mTOR pathway activated. In addition to signaling MPS,
this pathway also triggers the brain to release transmitters that blunt hunger and extend satiety.
Lastly, increased leucine levels in muscle tissue slow post-workout muscle breakdown caused by
the degrading effects of cortisol, a stress hormone.

quickly through the stomach and intestine and directly into the bloodstream for faster absorption.
