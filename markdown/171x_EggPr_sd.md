What was once the staple of bodybuilders everywhere, egg protein has recently taken a
"back seat" to whey. Nutritionists generally refer to egg as the "gold standard", or protein
to which all others should be compared, because of how well the body absorbs it and the
health promoting profile as a whole food. Obviously, some would argue that this opinion
is dated, in light of what we know about whey, but there's no denying that egg protein
does offer certain advantages. For starters, egg white protein is lactose-, fat-, and
cholesterol-free. Egg protein also contains high levels of sulfur, EAA’s, BCAA’s,
vitamins and electrolytes that athletes need.

Eggs contain all eight of the essential amino acids (the type that your body cannot make
from other substances) and very high levels of total amino acids - especially important
sulphur-containing aminos: cysteine and methionine. As a lactose-free protein source,
eggs are often more tolerable to people who have trouble with milk-based foods. For
these and other reasons, eggs have been a staple of bodybuilders’ diets since the
beginning of the sport.

Eggs also provide an excellent nutrition source for humans. Specifically, the quality and
bioavailability of egg white protein in human nutrition is considered ideal and represents
the standard by which other protein quality is measured. Egg albumin, found in egg
whites, is a complete protein and an excellent source of sulfur containing amino acids.
Egg protein has the longest history of use among weightlifters and bodybuilders due to its
high ratio of absorption and its time-proven results; not to mention that for many years it
was the only type of protein powder other than milk.

Egg white protein is considered ideal because it contains all the essential amino acids in
proper proportion for human nutrition. Egg protein is also easily digested thereby
enhancing the bioavailability of the protein making amino acids available for new protein
synthesis and replacement of lost protein.

For ages, athletes around the world looking for an excellent protein source have used egg
protein. Eggs are an important source of protein, essential vitamins and minerals and can
make a significant contribution to a healthy diet. While no one food (other than mother's
milk, perhaps) provides everything that human’s need, the egg contains a wide array of
necessary nutrients. Ultimate Nutrition’s Premium Egg Protein is an easy way to take in
all the egg protein an athlete wants in one delicious and nutritious shake.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS:

1. How should I use Egg Protein?

Egg protein powder can be used alone or as the ‘middle-man’ between whey and
casein, with whey absorbing ultra fast and casein ultra slow. Egg protein powder
fits in-between making it ideal for use in-between meals for a sustained protein
release of approximately 1-3 hours.

2. What is the difference between _albumin_ and _albumen_ ?

_Albumen_ comes from Latin _albus,_ “white,” and as early as the 16th century, it was
used to refer to the white of an egg. Chemists in the mid-1800s began to put a
finer edge to things, determining that the type of protein represented by egg
whites was part of a larger class of proteins that were chemically similar but had
different forms and functions. The protein present in the white of an egg came to
be called _albumin._

3. I love egg white but am concerned about the health aspects of using it.

For many years, eggs have gotten a bad rap as a forbidden food because of their
cholesterol content. Today, thanks to years of research, we know more than ever
about the relationship between diet, lifestyle and good health. There is growing
evidence that diet and health relationships are a function of both what is in the
diet and what is missing from it. It is also becoming clear that many of our
perceptions about various dietary factors are inaccurate. Egg white protein is
lactose-, fat-, and cholesterol-free and is a rich source of protein, containing the
essential amino acids the body requires.
