Glutamine accounts for over 20% of all circulating amino acids in humans, making it the
most prevalent in the body. Furthermore, it comprises more than 60% of the free amino
acid pool in skeletal muscle, with the remainder residing in the lungs, liver, brain, and
stomach tissue. During exhaustive exercise and physical training, glutamine is highly
utilized and levels can become depleted by as much as 50%. Thus, supplementation after
exercise may help reduce soreness and repair fatigued muscle tissue by reducing the
“cannibalization” of existing glutamine reserves.

In addition to maintaining muscle mass and impeding muscle breakdown, glutamine
supplementation may have several other benefits to bodybuilders and fitness buffs.
Glutamine enhances glycogen storage, which optimizes energy sources for muscle tissue,
resulting in improved strength and endurance. As it enters the Krebs Cycle, glutamine
converts to glutamate and produces adenosine triphosphate (ATP), a molecule that
transports energy within cells for metabolism. With adequate amounts of glutamine in the
body via diet and/or supplementation, less muscle tissue is broken down in the process of
supplying glucose. Increasing the availability of glucose to muscles during strenuous
physical activity via glutamine supplementation may increase exercise performance,
intensity, and recovery.

Glutamine also aids in the removal of nitrogen, lactic acid, pyruvate, and other
byproducts of energy metabolism from muscle tissue, thereby enhancing recovery.
Supplementation may also counter the effects of cortisol, the “stress hormone” secreted at
higher levels than normal during times of strenuous physical activity. Increased levels of
cortisol can diminish the body’s glutamine reserves.
