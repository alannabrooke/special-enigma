**FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS**


1. **What Foods Contain Vanadium?**

Foods rich in vanadium include shellfish, mushrooms, parsley and black pepper.
Beverages, fats and oils, and fresh fruits contain the least amount of vanadium, usually up
to 5 micrograms per gram weight. A microgram is 1/1000th of a milligram.
2. **What is the Difference Between Vanadium and Vanadyl Sulfate?**

Vanadium is the element found in nature. Vanadyl sulfate, on the other hand, is a "salt"
of vanadium, which is physiologically the more relevant form. In addition, the "salt" is
much better absorbed than elemental vanadium.


3. **What is the Daily Dosage of Vanadium?**

The daily dose requirement may vary from individual to individual depending upon the
extent to which vanadium must be replenished in the body for optimal health. On the
average, for a normal individual of average size and in reasonably good health, the
maximum daily dose should not exceed 10 mg at most. In cases of clinical deficiency, the
advice of a healthcare provider should be sought.
4. **What Are the Side Effects of Vanadium Supplementation?**

In large amounts, vanadium can be toxic. For an individual of average size and weight, a
daily dose not exceeding 10 mg should be safe. In case of any complicating factors or
doubt, the primary care physician should be consulted. Among the mild side effects are
diarrhea and loss of appetite. If these sign should appear with the minimum dosage,
competent clinical care should be sought as soon as possible.
